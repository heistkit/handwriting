Your handwriting, as a real font

Write it once.
Type it forever.

Copy a short sheet of characters onto blank paper, photograph it, and Inkwell builds an installable font in your own hand — with proper spacing, bold, italic, and letters that vary the way real writing does.

Everything runs inside your browser. Your handwriting is never uploaded, and this page works with the network switched off.

  • Spacing that actually works

    Each letter's bearings are measured from its own ink profile, so round, flat and diagonal shapes all end up looking equally spaced.

  • Letters that don't repeat

    Every character is built in three subtly different versions that rotate as you type, so "letter" doesn't show three identical t's.

  • Bold and italic included

    All four styles are generated from your single sample, so bold and italic work properly in any app.

  • Nothing leaves your device

    No account, no upload, no server. The font is built on your machine and saved straight to your downloads.

The process

Four steps, about ten minutes

  1. 1

    Copy the sheet

    The app shows you roughly 120 characters. Write them on any blank paper with any dark pen, leaving a gap between each one. No printer and no special paper needed.

  2. 2

    Photograph it

    Shoot straight down in ordinary light. Page rotation and uneven lighting are corrected automatically, so it does not need to be neat — only readable.

  3. 3

    Check and adjust

    Every character it found is shown back to you. Redraw any that came out wrong, then type a sentence and move the sliders until the spacing feels like your hand.

  4. 4

    Install it

    Download all four styles as one zip and install them like any other font. They share a family name, so Bold and Italic work normally in every app.

Under the hood

Why it doesn't look like a fake handwriting font

Three things separate a convincing handwriting font from the ones that read as a ransom note. All three are hard, and all three are handled.

Spacing is measured, not guessed

Most handwriting fonts give every letter the same margin. That looks wrong, because a round o already carries space in its own curve while a flat H carries none — identical margins make one look loose and the other cramped.

Inkwell measures the ink profile of every character and sets its bearings so the perceived gap comes out equal. On a real layout engine, ordinary prose shifts by under 1.5%, while a deliberately awkward line like AVATAR To Wave off; r. tightens by 5%.

No letter repeats itself

Type a double L in a typical handwriting font and you get the same shape stamped twice. Real writing never does that, and the eye catches it immediately.

Every character is built in three subtly different versions, rotated by a standard OpenType feature as you type — so the word letter shows three different t's. It stays on by default and can be switched off anywhere.

The baseline is solved, not assumed

Blank paper has no ruled line, so there is nothing to align letters to. Guess it and everything floats.

Because the app knows which characters it asked for, it knows that p drops below the line and k rises above it. Solving those constraints across a whole row at once recovers the baseline far more reliably than any single letter could — and it does it per row, so writing that drifts in size down the page still comes out even.

Bold and italic are real

Ask a word processor to bold a font that has no bold and it smears the outline sideways. It looks like exactly what it is.

Inkwell generates genuine Bold and Italic from your single sample — thickening outlines along their own edges, and leaning them about the middle of the x-height so the rhythm of the line survives. Markdown and rich text behave properly.

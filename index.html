Your handwriting, as a real font

Write it once.
Type it forever.

Copy a short sheet of characters onto blank paper and photograph it. Handwrite builds an installable font in your own hand: proper spacing, bold, italic, and letters that vary like real writing.

Everything runs inside your browser. Nothing you write or photograph leaves it, and there is no account.

What it does

  • Spacing that works

    Each letter's bearings are measured from its own ink profile, so round, flat and diagonal shapes look equally spaced.

  • Letters that don't repeat

    Every character is built in three subtly different versions that rotate as you type, so "letter" doesn't show three identical t's.

  • Bold and italic included

    All four styles are generated from your single sample, so bold and italic work properly in any app.

  • Nothing leaves your device

    No account, no upload, no server. The font is built on your machine and saved straight to your downloads.

The process

Four steps, about ten minutes

  1. Step 1 about 5 minutes

    Copy the sheet

    The app shows you about 120 characters. Write them on any blank paper with any dark pen, leaving a gap between them. No printer, no special paper.

  2. Step 2 about 1 minute

    Photograph it

    Shoot straight down in ordinary light. Page rotation and uneven lighting are corrected automatically; the photo only has to be readable.

  3. Step 3 about 3 minutes

    Check and adjust

    Every character it found is shown back to you. Redraw any that came out wrong, then type a sentence and move the sliders until the spacing feels like your hand.

  4. Step 4 about 1 minute

    Install it

    Download all four styles as one zip and install them like any other font. They share a family name, so Bold and Italic work normally in every app.

Under the hood

Why it doesn't look like a fake handwriting font

Four things separate a convincing handwriting font from the ones that read as a ransom note.

Spacing is measured

Most handwriting fonts give every letter the same margin. A round o already carries space in its own curve where a flat H carries none, so equal margins leave one loose and the other cramped.

Handwrite measures the ink profile of every character and sets its bearings so the perceived gap comes out equal. On a real layout engine, ordinary prose shifts by under 1.5%, while a deliberately awkward line like AVATAR To Wave off; r. tightens by 5%.

No letter repeats itself

Type a double L in a typical handwriting font and you get the same shape stamped twice. Real writing never does that, and the eye catches it immediately.

Every character is built in three subtly different versions, rotated as you type by a standard OpenType feature, so the word letter shows three different t's. It is on by default and can be switched off anywhere.

The baseline is solved

Blank paper has no ruled line, so there is nothing to align letters to. Guess it and everything floats.

The app knows which characters it asked for: p drops below the line, k rises above it. Solving those constraints across a whole row at once recovers the baseline far more reliably than any single letter could, and it happens per row, so writing that drifts in size down the page still comes out even.

Bold and italic are real

Ask a word processor to bold a font that has no bold and it smears the outline sideways.

Handwrite builds Bold and Italic from your single sample: outlines thickened along their own edges, and leaned about the middle of the x-height so the line keeps its rhythm. Markdown and rich text behave properly.

Ready when you are

All you need is paper, a pen, and a camera.

Copy these onto blank paper

Any paper, any dark pen. Write each character once, left to right, with a clear gap between them, and keep the rows well separated. The first sheet is enough on its own — about thirty characters, and you have a font. Come back for the others whenever you like.
